When competing priorities pull in different directions

Values are not just words on a list. They are qualities you want to express in how you live, and they can conflict. Independence may compete with stability; ambition may compete with time for family. The task is to acknowledge the trade-off rather than pretend every priority can be maximized at once.

Translate values into observable choices

Consider a week in your calendar. Where did your time go? Which commitments were chosen, and which were inherited? A useful exercise is to select two values and define one behaviour that expresses each. This makes the gap between aspiration and practice easier to examine.

How coaching helps

Together we can identify the decision or commitment where values are most relevant, consider practical constraints and explore a choice that you can live with. The goal is not to impose a hierarchy of values but to help you articulate your own.

Try this for yourself

  1. Pick three values you want to express.
  2. Write one recent action that reflected each.
  3. Identify a realistic adjustment for the coming week.

Questions people ask

Do my values have to stay the same?

No. Their importance can change with circumstances and life stages.

Is this the same as purpose coaching?

It overlaps, but focuses specifically on the principles behind concrete choices.

Coaching is a reflective and practical process; it is not a substitute for medical, mental-health, financial or legal advice. No specific outcome is guaranteed.
