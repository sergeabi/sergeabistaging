How to Choose a Life Coach

A practical guide to assessing fit, scope, transparency and boundaries before booking coaching.

Book a free introductory callTry the Crossroads Audit

Start with the problem you want help with

Before comparing coaches, describe the situation you want to explore. Is it a career decision, a recurring habit, an unclear direction or a need for mental-health treatment? Different professionals serve different purposes, and coaching is not appropriate for every problem.

Questions worth asking

Ask about the coach's relevant training and experience, their approach, confidentiality and its limits, fees, cancellation terms and what happens between sessions. Be cautious about guaranteed outcomes, pressure to buy a long package or claims that coaching can replace clinical care. A credible answer should be specific enough to evaluate.

Use the introductory call well

Notice whether you have space to ask questions, whether the coach explains boundaries and whether the proposed format suits your budget and schedule. You do not need to decide on the call. Compare alternatives if you are uncertain, including whether another kind of support is more appropriate.

Try this for yourself

  1. Write down your main coaching goal.
  2. Prepare three questions about scope and fees.
  3. After the call, note what was clear and what remains uncertain.

Questions people ask

Are credentials enough?

Credentials are one useful signal, alongside scope, experience, ethics and fit.

Should I buy a package immediately?

Only if you understand the terms and believe the format suits your needs.

Coaching is a reflective and practical process; it is not a substitute for medical, mental-health, financial or legal advice. No specific outcome is guaranteed.
