The Art of Play — Private 1:1 Workshop

Bring back curiosity, lightness and childlike wonder without abandoning adult responsibility.

Book a free introductory callTry the Crossroads Audit

Plan like an adult. Believe like a child.

Somewhere in adulthood we learn to treat seriousness as proof of responsibility. The calendar fills, curiosity becomes a luxury, and joy is scheduled for when the important work is finished. The Art of Play asks a different question: can you keep adult discernment while making room for wonder?

Inspired by the balance between practical planning and childlike faith found in Jim Rohn's teachings and wider wisdom traditions, this private 1:1 experience invites you to experiment with play as a way of relating to ordinary life.

The adult brings the structure

A childlike spirit doesn't mean ignoring bills, relationships or commitments. The adult in us can protect time, establish boundaries, assess risk and turn an idea into an action. These skills make exploration more sustainable, not less joyful.

In the session, we may look at where you have over-engineered your days, where you need more support, and what a realistic container for experimentation could look like.

The child brings the wonder

Curiosity can interrupt automatic thinking. Play can make failure less final. A willingness to try may help you reconnect with things you loved before you were concerned about being impressive or productive.

We might explore memories of spontaneous joy, movement, simple creativity, laughter or the kinds of experiences that make time feel spacious. There is no test to pass or personality to perform.

Leave with a playful experiment

The workshop is not an exercise in pretending difficulties don't exist. It is about making one small space for delight alongside difficulty. Together we can design an experiment that fits your energy, responsibilities and interests.

You might plan an unstructured afternoon, reconnect with a forgotten hobby, take a new route through your neighborhood or invite someone to do something wonderfully unnecessary.

Try this for yourself

  1. Remember an activity you enjoyed before you cared whether you were good at it.
  2. Find a 30-minute window for it this week, with no productivity goal.
  3. Afterward, notice how you felt—not whether you achieved anything.
