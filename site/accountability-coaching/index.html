Why commitments sometimes fail

A missed commitment can reveal that a goal is unclear, too large, poorly timed or not truly chosen. Accountability is most useful when it helps you understand the obstacle rather than adding shame. Start with something specific enough to observe.

Create a workable review rhythm

Define an action, the context in which you will do it and a simple way to tell whether it happened. At the next review, ask what supported or interrupted the action. If necessary, reduce the scope, change the environment or reconsider the goal. The process should support autonomy, not dependence.

How we can work together

Private coaching can help you design and review commitments. If you want continuity, the eight-week Limitless Protocol includes structured weekly check-ins. The exact arrangements should be agreed before you begin.

Try this for yourself

  1. Choose one action for the next seven days.
  2. Specify when and where it will happen.
  3. Set a review date and write what you learned.

Questions people ask

Will you monitor me every day?

Daily monitoring is not promised; the review rhythm is agreed together.

What if I miss a commitment?

We examine what happened and adjust rather than treating it as a personal failure.

Coaching is a reflective and practical process; it is not a substitute for medical, mental-health, financial or legal advice. No specific outcome is guaranteed.
