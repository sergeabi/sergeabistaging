The journey · Serge Abi

Leaving the expected route

There was a time when career progress seemed straightforward: study, work hard, build credibility and keep moving upward. I followed much of that route through business and consulting. From the outside, it offered recognizable signs of success. On the inside, I was questioning the cost.

My decision to leave conventional work in 2018 was not a declaration that careers are meaningless. It was an acknowledgment that the particular life I was living no longer felt like mine. I needed space to examine that honestly.

The road didn't provide all the answers

Travel opened conversations, landscapes and ways of living I would not have encountered from an office. It also exposed uncertainty, discomfort and the limits of trying to solve an inner question through an outer change.

Over time, I began to see that freedom is less about keeping every option open than about choosing what deserves commitment. Relationships, health, purpose and the capacity to be present came into sharper focus.

From exploration to contribution

The next chapter brought together entrepreneurial projects, meditation, community and the wish to support others facing a transition. My work today has a practical side: building and advising projects. It also has a reflective side: helping people ask better questions about the life they're building.

I don't teach a formula for quitting your job and becoming free. That would ignore differences in privilege, finances, responsibilities and personality. I offer lessons and questions you can adapt to your circumstances.

Freedom as a continuing practice

There are days when clarity arrives easily and days when old habits return. The work is to notice, recalibrate and choose again. An ordinary day lived with attention can be freer than a spectacular journey lived on autopilot.

If you're considering a transition, start by understanding what you want to move toward—not only what you want to escape. Then design a first experiment that respects your real life.

Try this for yourself

  1. Describe one assumption about success you inherited.
  2. Ask what it has given you and what it costs you now.
  3. Identify one practical change that could bring your life closer to your current values.
