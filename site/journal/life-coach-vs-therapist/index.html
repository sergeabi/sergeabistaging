Life Coach vs Therapist: What's the Difference?

Understand coaching and therapy, their limits and how to choose appropriate support.

Take the Crossroads AuditExplore coaching

Reflection · 4 November 2026

Coaching and therapy answer different kinds of needs

A life coach generally works with goals, decisions, accountability and personal development. A therapist is a qualified mental-health professional who can assess and treat psychological conditions within their training and legal scope. The labels are not interchangeable, and professional regulation varies by country.

Understanding the distinction helps you choose support that matches your situation. Coaching may be useful when you're functioning reasonably well but want to clarify a transition or make a difficult choice. Therapy may be more appropriate when mental-health symptoms, trauma or emotional distress need clinical attention.

What happens in life coaching?

A coaching conversation might explore what you want, the obstacles you perceive, the commitments you're ready to make and what you learned from a recent experiment. The coach can ask questions, offer reflection and help organize next steps.

A competent coach should be clear about scope and avoid presenting personal-development exercises as a treatment for depression, anxiety or trauma. Credentials and methods vary, so ask about training and experience before committing.

What happens in therapy?

Depending on the professional and approach, therapy may involve assessment, treatment planning, evidence-based interventions and support with emotional or behavioral difficulties. Some therapists also help people work toward life goals, so there can be overlap in topics.

The critical distinction is professional scope and whether clinical assessment or treatment is needed. When in doubt, consulting a licensed mental-health professional is a sensible starting point.

How to choose responsibly

Consider the nature, duration and impact of what you're experiencing. If persistent distress is affecting sleep, safety, functioning or relationships, seek clinical advice. In an emergency or if you may harm yourself, contact local emergency services or crisis support.

You can also use coaching and therapy at different times—or sometimes alongside each other with appropriate boundaries. Be wary of anyone promising a guaranteed cure or discouraging legitimate medical care.

Try this for yourself

  1. Describe the outcome you want from seeking support.
  2. Ask a potential practitioner about qualifications, scope, confidentiality and referral practices.
  3. If symptoms significantly affect daily functioning, seek assessment from a qualified health professional.

Questions people ask

Can a life coach diagnose mental-health conditions?

No. Diagnosis and treatment must be provided by appropriately qualified professionals.

Could coaching complement therapy?

Sometimes, depending on the needs and professional guidance involved. Clear scope and communication matter.
