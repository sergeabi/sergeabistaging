How to Enjoy Life Again as an Adult

Rediscover simple pleasure, play and connection without pressuring yourself to feel happy.

Take the Crossroads AuditExplore coaching

Reflection · 4 November 2026

You don't need to earn permission to enjoy yourself

Adulthood can turn pleasure into a reward you receive only after every task is complete. The list never ends, so joy gets postponed indefinitely. If you recognize that pattern, the first change may be to stop treating delight as something frivolous.

Enjoyment is often modest: an unhurried walk, a conversation that isn't about work, making something badly for fun, music in the kitchen, or a moment of rest without checking your phone. These experiences matter because they are part of life, not distractions from it.

Make room for curiosity

Ask what you used to enjoy before you were concerned about outcomes. Was it drawing, dancing, hiking, exploring new neighborhoods, laughing with friends or learning something simply because it was interesting? You don't need to become excellent at any of it.

Play is valuable partly because it loosens the demand to perform. Give yourself a short window in which the process matters more than the result.

Connection changes the texture of a day

Sometimes the missing ingredient isn't another hobby but another human being. Make space for shared experiences that aren't purely transactional. Ask a friend for a walk, invite someone to cook together or join a group around a genuine interest.

If you've felt isolated, start gently. A brief, low-pressure interaction may be easier than committing to a large social event.

Take persistent loss of pleasure seriously

If little or nothing feels enjoyable for a prolonged period, especially alongside low mood or changes in sleep, energy or appetite, it may be a sign that additional support is needed. A healthcare professional can help assess what is happening.

There is no moral failure in needing care. Playful experiments can complement a healthy life, but they aren't a substitute for assessment or treatment when appropriate.

Try this for yourself

  1. Make a list of ten small experiences you once enjoyed.
  2. Pick one that takes less than thirty minutes and requires no special purchase.
  3. Notice whether sharing it with someone changes the experience.

Questions people ask

What if I don't feel like doing anything fun?

Start very small, without forcing a feeling. If loss of interest persists or affects daily life, seek professional support.

Do I need an expensive hobby?

No. Many enjoyable activities are simple, social or free.
