When your career no longer feels like your own

A career change is more than choosing a new job title. Work can determine your income, identity, routines, location and relationships. That's why feeling stuck professionally may also feel like a life crisis, even if the job looks good from the outside.

I know how persuasive a respectable path can be. My own shift away from consulting began with recognizing a mismatch between the life I was building and the life I wanted to experience. Your choice may look very different: a new role, a redesigned schedule, a side project or a careful exit.

Don't confuse urgency with clarity

A stressful week can make quitting feel like the only path to relief. Before making irreversible decisions, take stock of your finances, commitments, transferable skills and alternatives. Identify what is wrong with the current role and what you hope a change would actually solve.

We can distinguish a harmful environment from a mismatch of values, or a need for rest from a need for a different profession. These questions deserve practical analysis as much as emotional honesty.

Build a bridge rather than a cliff

Many transitions become safer and more informative when tested. Speak with people in the field you want to explore, take on a limited project, study an unfamiliar skill or renegotiate one part of your working week. These steps can reveal what the next chapter would really demand.

The point isn't always to move slowly; it's to make informed choices. We can map the decision you face and decide which uncertainty is worth resolving first.

What coaching can contribute

A private session can provide structured reflection on career identity, trade-offs, fears and next steps. For professional decisions requiring specialist expertise—employment law, financial planning or medical leave—consult the relevant professional as well.

You don't need to have a resignation letter ready to begin. Sometimes the most useful move is understanding what should remain and what must change.

Try this for yourself

  1. Write what you want to leave behind and what you hope to move toward.
  2. List three costs of staying and three costs of leaving.
  3. Plan one informational conversation before committing to a major change.

Questions people ask

Do I need to quit my job to work with you?

No. Coaching can support reflection before any job change, including decisions to stay and renegotiate.

Can you provide career placement or legal advice?

No. This is reflective coaching, not recruitment or employment-law advice.

Coaching is a reflective and practical process; it is not a substitute for medical, mental-health, financial or legal advice. No specific outcome is guaranteed.
